# Inspiration House

The taste ledger. It turns what Krish saves and judges into a record of **why** he likes it, asks him to confirm the patterns, and gives the confirmed ones to the systems that write and produce for him, so their output gets closer to his taste every week.

## What it knows about his taste

Three layers, each built from the one before:

1. **His words, kept whole.** Whatever he typed (a card answer, a verdict, a correction, a line he holds up, a report of what he said in another session) is kept as he wrote it, on its card and in `taste_evidence`. An excerpt cut to fit keeps its whole text beside it (`meta.full_text`). The long form is the context every later reading starts from, so nothing he said is ever summarised away.
2. **Signals: his words as tags you can count** (`signals` stage, `taste_signals`). Each answer is split into every separate thing he responded to, and each becomes one tag from a fixed vocabulary (`src/signal/vocab.ts`): the craft layer or other dimension it is about, the slug, likes or dislikes, how strongly he said it, any creator or piece he compared it to, the phrase of his it rests on, and where it applies (lane, source, platform, format, kind). Counted only by SQL, in `taste_signal_patterns` and `taste_signal_patterns_by_context`: how many of his answers back each tag, from how many places, trending up or down, and where his taste pulls both ways. Whenever the reader improves, every past answer is read again.
3. **Whys and claims: the rules he confirms** (`explain`, `distil`, `propose`). Each answer also gives one to three plain-sentence whys; the weekly distil groups them into claims, each a rule a writer could apply tomorrow, and puts at most three a week to him. Only a rule he approves or rewords reaches the systems that write for him.

Signals are the pattern store; claims are the decisions. A signal never decides anything and never reaches a reader of approved taste.

It is headless: no UI of its own and nothing to host. It runs on GitHub Actions, keeps its state in the Mindmaker OS Supabase project, and is read by these consumers:

- **content-engine** reads approved claims while drafting and revising (never while judging or ranking).
- **ai-harness**'s `krish-voice` and `krish-design` skills read them from a file each, which arrives only by a pull request Krish merges (`pnpm engine harness`).
- **Sessions in every repository** read the approved form rules of their own venture through Control Center's `get_approved_taste` tool, and report back through its `report_to_taste_engine` tool (the `reports` stage below). How that two-way path was built: `docs/TWO_WAY.md`.
- **Control Center** shows the Inspiration page (one card per thing saved in Drive), and its Visibility scorer reads the cards he routed to "A room or stage to go for" as the kind of room he wants. An answer with that route counts as visibility-lane evidence here, whichever folder the card came from.

**Each venture reads only its own taste.** Krish, 2026-10-28: "each repository builds its own taste ledger and reads from its own taste ledger, and not the central makeyourmindup one", and "The ledger is keyed by venture not repository. Makeyourmindup as a separate venture to mindmake". So every row of the ledger carries one venture (a `venture_registry` slug: Make Your Mind Up is `publication`, Mindmake is `mindmake`, and so on), decided in code by `src/venture.ts` and never by a model: content-engine's taste files, the Drive folders, the Inspiration page, the idea queue and the Rejections folder are Make Your Mind Up's; a visibility target is its product's (Mindmake's when it has none); Nova's and Nell's old feedback is Mindmake's; a session's report is its repository's venture (content-engine's `taste_venture_repositories`), or, from a repository that serves several, the venture the session named. A report with no venture is held, counted as `reports_unassigned`, and fails the run until it has one. There is no shared tier and no fallback: a venture with no approved rule reads none, never Make Your Mind Up's. The rendered skill files hold one section per venture, headed by its repositories, and a session applies only its own.

It also reads what **content-watcher** leaves behind. When Krish pastes a link into the box on the Inspiration board, Control Center queues it (`content_watch_requests`), content-watcher on one of his home computers fetches the post, files its media into his Drive folder and reads it into text (`content_reads`, one reading per file, found by the file's sha256). This engine then makes the card, links the request to it, and tags it from that reading.

## How it works

| Stage | When | What |
|---|---|---|
| collect | daily | Reads, never writes, the places Krish's taste already lands: content-engine's taste files (learnings, title verdicts, golden and rejected lines, style references), idea triage, visibility decisions, creator moves, drive-scan's artifacts, the What I like sheet, both Drive folders, the Instagram workbook in Cold Ideas (each post as an observation, each theme as a machine inference; context, never proof), and his answers on the Inspiration page. Whatever he typed on a card is his words; an answer that is only taps is his action. An answer he takes back or changes stops counting: each run withdraws the page's older versions and the claims citing them are recounted. The Instagram workbook is read whole too: a post's version no longer in the workbook (an edit, or a re-uploaded workbook, whose new file id gives every post a new version) is withdrawn, so each post stands once; a read that finds no rows withdraws nothing. |
| cues | daily | Turns what Krish said about an idea or a saved post into a feedback cue the Sunday session draws on (`src/collect/feedbackCues.ts`, content-engine's `feedback_cues`): his words verbatim, the subject they are about, and what they are for: `future_piece` (make it later), `wider_piece_context` (context for a bigger piece, grouped by the theme he names), `track_over_time`, `brief` (how to tell it) or `not_for_us`. It reads his idea-queue verdicts (current and history, `decided_by: 'Krish'` only, and only an answer with his words), his typed card answers (`why_here`, `would_do`, and `why_like` when typed) and his presses on the publish board (content-engine's `board_documents`, only those he wrote himself, `updated_by: 'Krish'`). A queue press that names a use (`wider_piece_context`, `track_over_time`, `next_week`, `not_for_us`) is his tap, so its cue takes that use with `use_origin` `krish` and `decided_by: 'Krish'` in its meta; `next_week` is filed as `future_piece`. Which week a next_week idea is queued for lives only on its queue mark (`target_week` in content-engine's `content_ideas.meta.queue`), which the Sunday gather orders by; the cue's `meta.target_week` is context, the Monday the press named, never guessed. A press joins its idea's queue answers, so the answer in the queue that relays the same words, given after the press, is never read again by the verdict rules. A pick with a note is a `week_pick` cue, his brief for the piece he picked, read so by rule (`use_origin` `rule`: the tap names a day); every pick also counts as his yes, so it retires an earlier `not_for_us`. An `offday` press is Growth's and is only counted, and a press with no words makes no cue but still retires what it contradicts. A press that cannot be filed fails the run while its board is live, or once when it is new, and is counted after (`cues_board_unfiled`); a queue document of his with words but no use this reader knows is counted (`cues_board_unread`). The use comes from rules first: his press (`use_origin` `krish`, a pick's note `rule`), then his words naming it (`krish`), then his verdict or the card's route (`rule`); only where no rule fires does the model guess (`model_guess`, with its model and prompt revision), and a guess on unchanged words is reused, never asked again. A changed answer updates its cue, a later drop retires the idea's other cues, an answer that is gone retires its cue, and a cue a piece used is never touched. Board comments are not read: their anchors map to no idea or card, and his QUEUE comments already reach the queue. Until the `feedback_cues` migration is applied it writes nothing and counts `cues_table_missing`. |
| reports | daily | Files what a session in any repository told the engine through Control Center's `report_to_taste_engine` tool (`src/collect/sessionReports.ts`, content-engine's `taste_reports`). His exact typed words, with where and when he typed them, become `krish_words` evidence (source `session_report`), which can count toward a claim like anything else he said; a report of his words that does not say where or when is refused and filed as nothing. Anything else a session reports (a paraphrase, a summary, what it noticed, how a piece did) becomes `observation` evidence: context only, never explained and never counted. Where his words name what to do with an idea or a card (by the cues stage's word rules only, no model), the report also becomes a feedback cue (source `session_report`). Each report is filed once and never edited. Until the `taste_reports` migration is applied it reads nothing and counts `reports_table_missing`. |
| tag | daily | Settles each card's kind (`src/tag/kinds.ts`): a video, an image, a feed that keeps itself fresh, a research corpus, an idea or script, or another document. The file settles it when it can, the model reads a document it cannot, and a kind Krish sets on the page is never overwritten. A video's guesses are about its script, narrative or production style, an image's about its clarity; a clear opinion about AI rides on any card as its topic; a corpus or an idea names the piece on the slate it likely serves (a guess he confirms). A feed is never asked anything and goes to no model: its posts are read row by row (collect). A feed never gives taste evidence. A corpus gives none for naming its piece (or a new one), but anything else Krish gives on any card counts, whatever its kind: his words, his picks, tags he confirmed or another place he sends it, because a kind can be the model's guess and a guess never takes his answer away. Only "Make a piece from it" with nothing else looks the same as naming a new piece, so it stays out. Then gives each Drive item a card: a thumbnail, guessed tags, guessed one-tap answers, and facets (platform, format, source, creator, people, organisations, topics), with what the file name proves kept as fact (`src/tag/hints.ts`). A video is watched: eight frames spread across it and its words transcribed on the runner (`src/tag/watch.ts`, ffmpeg and faster-whisper), the words kept on the card. Whether the words were heard (`words`, `silent`, `too_big` for a file over the 150 MB fetch cap, or `failed` with its class) is kept on the card's facets with its tries. A run in which a try fails is a failed run; an unheard video is tried again on the next run, up to three tries (`HEAR_TRIES`), and a try on a card already tagged only writes the hearing, with no model call, unless it heard words. Words heard once stay on the card and are never heard again: a card tagged again takes only its frames, and when the model call fails after the words were heard, the next run tags the card from them. Tagging starts no new card after 12 minutes, so explaining and the run's record always run inside the job's hour. A card tagged under an older revision (`TAG_PROMPT_REV`) is tagged again, and so is a card he told how to read (read_as, or a note for Claude) after it was tagged, once. Only cards whose file is in Drive that run are tagged. One card's model error fails that card only. A guess stays a guess until he answers. A file content-watcher has already read is never downloaded or watched here: it is tagged from that reading (up to 30,000 characters of it, given to the model as data, never as instructions) and Drive's thumbnail, its words become the card's transcript, and the card says it was read that way (`facets.read_from`, `facets.read_rev`). A reading that could not hear a video counts as one of its three tries, so the card stops coming back after the third run. A reading that does not say whether it heard a video counts it as heard only when it has a section of the words said (`## Words (...)`). A file with no reading is watched as before. |
| link | daily, and the cards job | Links each pasted link to the card its media became (`src/tag/links.ts`). Drive gives each file's sha256, and the request lists the sha256 of every file content-watcher filed, so the files are matched by that hash (in either case) and their cards by Drive id. The request's card is the card of the first file, in the request's order, that has one; the request then points at it (`item_id`) and the card carries the link (`facets.link`: the request, the link, its platform, the author and date when the reading says them, when he pasted it, and how many files the post had). A request linked before its reading arrived gets the author and date on a later run, once it points at its reading; what a card already says is never overwritten. A carousel is one card: every other slide's card points at it (`facets.part_of`, with its place in the post), Control Center hides those, and the tagger never sends them to the model. A card another post already took is never taken again: when the same post is pasted under a second address, or a single picture is already a slide of another post, the second request points at that post's card and changes nothing on it. The note he typed when he pasted the link becomes the card's "why he saved it" only when the card has none; the card still asks its question, and the note counts as his words from the moment he pasted it. Until he answers the card, the note follows his request: when he pastes the link again with a new note, the card takes the new one, and after his Undo on the page, which empties "why he saved it", his note comes back on the next run. A card he has answered is never changed by a note. A second run changes nothing. The logs carry only how many requests were linked, slides folded and notes kept. |
| explain | daily | Turns each piece of his evidence into one to three candidate whys, each tagged form (craft) or subject. Each piece is read once (`explained_at`), even when it gives no why; withdrawn evidence is never read. A card's kind is given to it as fact only when the file proves it or Krish set it; a kind the tagger guessed is labelled its guess (`kindLine`). |
| signals | daily | Breaks each piece of his evidence into countable taste signals (`src/signal/extract.ts`, content-engine's `taste_signals`), so patterns in his taste can be counted over months. A why is one sentence for a claim to cite; a signal is one tag, and a long answer of his gives many. Each tag comes from a fixed vocabulary (`src/signal/vocab.ts`): a dimension (one of the craft layers, or `subject`, or `workflow` for how he wants the system to work with him) and a slug, or `other` with a short label. The `other`s are counted every run, so the vocabulary grows by a pull request he merges and never sprawls. Each signal carries its polarity (he can like the hook of something he rejects), a strength from 1 to 3, a creator or piece he names as the comparison, and the phrase of his words it rests on. Code keeps that phrase only when it is his words character for character (`origin` `krish`), and so does the database; otherwise the signal is the model's guess (`model_guess`) and keeps none. A tapped answer gives `krish_action` signals. Where it applies (lane, source, and a card's platform, format, and kind when the file proves it or he set it) is copied from the ledger and the card, never asked of the model. The model reads the whole of his words: an excerpt cut to fit (400 characters for most sources) keeps its whole text in `meta.full_text`, out of the row's version, so no row becomes a new version, and a row written before gets it on the next run. Each answer is read once per prompt revision (`SIGNAL_PROMPT_REV`, in `taste_signal_reads`); a new revision reads the backlog again and supersedes the older readings, so a better reader improves every past answer. At most 200 answers a run, newest first. The counts are SQL's: `taste_signal_patterns` gives each tag's support from his signals only (answers, sources, how many rest on his phrase, strength, the last 90 days against the 90 before, and the answers on the same tag with the other polarity), with the guesses beside it, never in it; `taste_signal_patterns_by_context` splits it by lane, platform, format and kind. Withdrawn answers drop out. Until the `taste_signals` migration is applied, a real run writes nothing and asks no model, and counts `signals_table_missing`; a dry run still reads, and its preview shows the signals, their patterns and the `other` labels. |
| distil | weekly | Groups whys into claims, one venture at a time: each venture's claims rest only on its own whys (the database refuses a claim citing another venture's row), the model is told which venture it is reading, and a venture with fewer than three of his rows behind its whys is skipped. The model drafts; code checks every citation; the database counts the support. Only a candidate is rewritten, and a superseded one drafted again comes back as a candidate: once a claim is put to Krish, its words, evidence and quote never change, while he decides or after, enforced by the database. |
| propose | weekly | Puts at most three claims a week to Krish, across all ventures (the cap limits his attention), through the existing learning-proposals door, each naming its venture (`scope.venture`). He decides; the engine never does. |
| harness | daily | Renders the approved form rules into ai-harness's `skills/krish-voice/references/approved-taste.md` and `skills/krish-design/references/approved-taste.md` and, when they differ from ai-harness `main`, opens or refreshes one pull request there (branch `taste/approved-rules`). An open pull request that already holds the set is left alone, a rule he reworded carries only his words, and a set he closed without merging is never proposed again (its hash is in the pull request's body). Nothing reaches those skills until Krish merges it (Ruling, Krish, 2026-10-24: rules arrive by PR). |
| rejections | daily, its own job | The floor beside the ceiling (`src/rejection/`, `taste-rejections.yml`). Krish, 2026-10-28: "a separate side engine called Rejections (where all the working rejected production work goes, with the reason why I rejected it - acting as the floor, whilst Inspiration acts as the Ceiling)". It reads his Rejections folder in Drive by id, whole (`data/rejections.jsonl`, `liked.jsonl`, `process.jsonl`, `_manifest.json`), plus the dislikes sessions report through Control Center (`session_report` evidence) and the capture inbox (`taste_rejection_inbox`). The stable unit is his words: one `taste_rejection_quotes` row per distinct thing he said (its normalised text and day), so a dump renumbered by a later merge pass files nothing twice; a record (`taste_rejections`) is keyed by the quotes it rests on, never by the dump's R id. A quote is his words only when dated and marked verbatim; approving a session's critique is his action; a paraphrase is an observation. Each record is placed by rule (`src/rejection/vocab.ts`, `RULES_REV`): a theme, the makers it binds (`short_video`, `cover`, `script` ...), and how its defect is kept: a still, a moment, a whole cut, or the words alone. Of each asset it keeps a still or a six-frame strip (at most 400 KB) or, around a moment, a 4 s low-res clip and a strip cut on the runner with ffmpeg (at most 1.6 MB), and lets the master go; a whole cut is pointed at by its master's Drive id. The database refuses anything larger, so no master is ever kept. No frame or clip goes to a model (Krish, 2026-10-24). Then the model drafts the standards per group of makers from his words, text only, when the records change; code checks every citation and quote, the database counts support (distinct quotes of his in standing records); each standard is weighed by the productions it was raised about: a pattern (two or more) is more critical to follow than a one-off and comes first in the file (Krish, 2026-10-28: "find patterns, so we can cement certain rejections as much being more critical to follow than a one off complaint about one production"); with `--propose` the patterns go to him through `mindmake_studio_learning_proposals` in one batch (Ruling, Krish, 2026-10-28), never under the ceiling's three-a-week cap, and a one-off goes once it recurs. The ceiling (Inspiration) is not weighed this way: he is testing new formats and looks there. Last it renders `config/rejection-floor.json` and proposes it to content-engine by pull request (branch `floor/rejections`, `FLOOR_SYNC_TOKEN`), where every session reads it with `scripts/floor.py --format <maker>`. The file holds his words, never a PC path, Drive path or id, session id or email: the render refuses one that does. Subject records (what a piece is about) are kept and never served (anti-echo). Until the `rejections` migration is applied, a real run writes nothing and fails, and a dry run still reads the folder and previews the file. |
| score | weekly | Computes the scoreboard. The moat metric is corrections per published piece: lower means the output needed him less. |

## Rules it keeps

- **Never writes to Drive.** Google scopes are read-only, checked in CI.
- **His words are kept whole.** An excerpt is a cut for display; the whole of what he typed stays beside it (`meta.full_text`), out of the row's version, and every reader that needs it (the signals stage) reads all of it.
- **A tag is only countable if it is always called the same thing.** The signals vocabulary lives in `src/signal/vocab.ts` and grows only by a pull request: the model picks a slug or says `other`, and each run counts the `other` labels (in the dry run's preview) so the next slug to add is plain. Changing a slug's meaning means a new `VOCAB_REV`; changing the reader means a new `SIGNAL_PROMPT_REV`, which reads the backlog again.
- **His words are his.** A claim can quote him only verbatim, enforced by the database, and a reader shows the quote only while the answer it quotes stands: when he gives that answer back, his quote comes back with it. A tapped suggestion is recorded as his endorsement, never as his words.
- **Taste shapes form, never subject** (anti-echo). Readers only see `taste_profile_form`.
- **Each venture's taste is its own.** A row's venture is set in code (`src/venture.ts`) and never changes; every compiling step reads one venture, and every reader filters by its own. The floor is Make Your Mind Up's only for now: every venture's rejections are filed under their venture, and only Make Your Mind Up's are distilled, proposed and rendered, since only it has a floor reader. Each run counts `venture_mismatch`, the stored rows whose venture differs from what the code gives them now, and fails on one.
- **A model answer is read here, never by the SDK.** The API never receives a schema's length or count limits, so an answer that runs over one is repaired and counted (`src/repair.ts`), never thrown away; an answer that is the wrong shape still fails that one item.
- **Numbers are computed, never emitted by a model.** Support, confidence and every score come from SQL.
- **Silence is not feedback.** An unanswered proposal stays proposed, and a quiet week scores as no reading, never zero.
- **This repository is public.** Nothing Krish said is committed or logged; logs carry counts only (`src/log.ts`). Fixtures are synthetic.

## The schema

The tables live in **content-engine** (`supabase/migrations/`), which owns content DDL, and are applied with the Supabase MCP `apply_migration`, never `db push`. This engine reads a run of its migrations, starting at `20261104090000_taste_ledger.sql`. The ordered list, with what each one is for, is in the header of `test/pipeline.int.test.ts`, which applies them: that header is the one list. The cues, reports and signals stages write nothing until their own migrations (`20261111090000_feedback_cues.sql`, `20261113090000_taste_reports.sql`, `20261115090000_taste_signals.sql`) are applied; the rejections job needs `20261116090000_rejections.sql`; every other stage needs the ledger. Every stage needs `20261117090000_taste_ventures.sql`, which keys every row by venture.

## Running it

```bash
pnpm install
pnpm test && pnpm typecheck
# The database tests (test/pipeline.int.test.ts) need a local UTF8 Postgres and content-engine's migrations; CI
# (.github/workflows/ci.yml) runs them on every pull request and every push to main or a claude/ branch, and the test
# file's header says how to run them by hand.
pnpm engine daily --dry-run --preview    # read-only; a readable preview goes to preview/, which is gitignored
pnpm engine weekly --dry-run --preview
pnpm engine rejections --dry-run --preview  # the floor: reads the Rejections folder, previews config/rejection-floor.json
```

A dry run refuses every write. Without `DATABASE_URL` it reads through the Supabase Management API (`SUPABASE_ACCESS_TOKEN`). In a cloud session whose proxy holds the Supabase credential, route Node through it: `NODE_USE_ENV_PROXY=1 NODE_EXTRA_CA_CERTS=<the proxy's CA bundle> pnpm engine daily --dry-run --preview`.

A daily dry run with the model on shows the signals stage's work in its preview: every signal it would keep, the patterns they add up to (`patternsOf`, the same grouping as the view), and the `other` labels to grow the vocabulary by.

## Turning it on

1. Apply content-engine's migrations in the order listed in the header of `test/pipeline.int.test.ts`. The cues, reports and signals stages write nothing until their own migrations are applied.
2. Add repository secrets: `DATABASE_URL`, `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, `HARNESS_SYNC_TOKEN` (a fine-grained token with contents and pull request write on `krishanraja/ai-harness` only), and `GOOGLE_SERVICE_ACCOUNT_PRIVATE_KEY` holding the service account's whole JSON key file (or the bare PEM, with `GOOGLE_SERVICE_ACCOUNT_EMAIL` beside it). Share both Drive folders and the What I like sheet with the service account as Viewer, and for the rejections job the Rejections folder, 3 Posts and Video Engine > Archive. The rejections job also takes `FLOOR_SYNC_TOKEN` (contents and pull request write on `krishanraja/content-engine` only); without it the floor is rendered and counted but not proposed. The rejections schedule starts only once the repository variable `REJECTIONS_ENABLED` is `true` (set it after its migration, the Drive sharing and a first real run by hand), and a scheduled rejections run puts standards to Krish only once `FLOOR_PROPOSE_ENABLED` is `true`.
3. Run each workflow once by hand with `dry_run` on and `use_model` off: it proves every source is reachable and costs nothing. Then run it with both off for the first real run, and `use_model` on when it should tag and explain.
4. Set the repository variable `TASTE_ENABLED` to `true`. Until then the schedules do nothing.

### The cards job

`pnpm -s engine cards [--dry-run] [--no-model]` (workflow `taste-cards.yml`, run by hand or by Control Center's knock, which starts it when a pasted link has been filed or read and has no card yet) is the quick path for a pasted link. It makes the missing cards, links the requests, and tags at most 10 linked cards, from their reading only, in a job of at most 15 minutes with no ffmpeg and no Python. Its 10 places go only to linked cards whose reading has arrived, so newer cards still waiting never crowd out an older one that is ready. A linked card whose reading has not arrived yet is left for the daily job and counted as `waiting_for_reading`. The daily job does the same linking, so a link never depends on the knock.

Runs are recorded in `content_engine_runs` as `taste_daily`, `taste_weekly`, `taste_harness`, `taste_cards` and `taste_rejections`, where Control Center's done-check reads them.
